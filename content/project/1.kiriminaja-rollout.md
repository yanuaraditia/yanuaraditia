## Bridging the UX Gap in Argo Rollouts

Releasing a new version of an application is never just a technical activity—it’s a cross-functional effort that involves Product, QA, and Engineering teams working in sync. While modern deployment tools have made it easier to ship code frequently, they often fall short when it comes to usability and control for non-engineering stakeholders.

Tools like Argo Rollouts are incredibly powerful. They provide advanced deployment strategies such as canary releases and blue-green deployments, enabling teams to minimize risk and gradually introduce changes. However, this power comes with a trade-off: the user experience is primarily designed for engineers who are comfortable working directly with Kubernetes resources and configurations.

For Product teams, this creates friction.

The challenge is not just about deploying safely—it’s about understanding _when_ to release, _who_ should approve, and _how_ to control each stage of the rollout without relying heavily on engineering intervention. In many organizations, this leads to informal processes, manual coordination, or even risky workarounds that bypass proper controls.

### The Missing Layer: UX and Access Control

During the development of KiriminAja Rollout Manager, we identified a key gap in the ecosystem: the lack of a structured and user-friendly way to manage rollout permissions and responsibilities.

Out of the box, Argo Rollouts does not provide an Access Control List (ACL) mechanism tailored to rollout actions. This means that anyone with sufficient cluster access could potentially trigger, pause, promote, or abort a rollout—regardless of their role within the team.

In practice, this creates several problems:

- **Lack of ownership clarity**: It’s not always clear who is responsible for advancing a rollout.
- **Operational risk**: Unauthorized or accidental actions can impact production systems.
- **Limited accountability**: Without clear role boundaries, it becomes harder to audit decisions.
- **Poor collaboration**: Product and QA teams are often dependent on engineers to perform simple rollout actions.

We realized that what was missing wasn’t another deployment strategy—but a **bridge**.

### Building the Bridge

KiriminAja Rollout Manager is designed as a bridge layer on top of Argo Rollouts—one that focuses on simplifying the user experience while introducing structured control over rollout actions.

Instead of replacing Argo Rollouts, we embrace it. We leverage its strengths and extend it with capabilities that make it more accessible to a broader set of users.

At the core of this bridge is a custom-built Access Control List (ACL) system.

This ACL system allows teams to define roles such as:

- Product Owner
- QA Engineer
- Developer
- Release Manager

Each role can be assigned specific permissions tied to different stages of the rollout lifecycle. For example:

- A **Product Owner** can approve or reject a full rollout to 100% of users.
- A **QA Engineer** can pause, resume, or analyze canary deployments during testing phases.
- A **Developer** can initiate a rollout but may not have permission to promote it to full release.
- A **Release Manager** can oversee and coordinate the entire process.

By explicitly defining who can do what, we reduce ambiguity and enforce a safer deployment workflow.

### Simplifying the Experience

Beyond access control, the goal of this bridge is to make rollout management more intuitive.

Instead of interacting with low-level configurations, users can:

- View rollout status in a more human-readable way
- Understand the current stage of deployment at a glance
- Perform actions through a guided interface
- Follow a structured approval flow

This shift transforms rollout management from a purely technical task into a collaborative workflow that aligns with how teams actually operate.

It also reduces the cognitive load on engineers, who no longer need to act as gatekeepers for every rollout action. Product and QA teams can take ownership of their respective responsibilities without compromising safety.

### Improving Safety and Compliance

Introducing an ACL layer is not just about convenience—it’s also about governance.

With clearly defined roles and permissions:

- Sensitive actions are restricted to authorized individuals
- Every action can be traced back to a responsible role
- Teams can align rollout processes with internal policies and compliance requirements

This becomes especially important as organizations scale, where the number of stakeholders and the complexity of releases increase significantly.

### Current Status and Future Plans

At the moment, KiriminAja Rollout Manager is a closed-source project. We are actively using and refining it internally to ensure it meets real-world needs and maintains a high standard of reliability.

That said, this is not intended to remain private forever.

We have plans to open source the project in the future, allowing the wider community to adopt, contribute to, and evolve this approach. We believe that improving the usability and safety of rollout processes is a shared challenge—and one that benefits from collaboration.

### Rethinking Rollouts

Ultimately, this project is not about replacing existing tools, but about enhancing them.

Argo Rollouts already solves the problem of _how_ to deploy safely at a technical level. What we are solving is _how teams interact with that power_—how they collaborate, make decisions, and maintain control throughout the release process.

By building a bridge that simplifies the UX and introduces structured access control, we enable teams to:

- Move faster without sacrificing safety
- Reduce dependency on engineering for operational tasks
- Improve accountability and transparency
- Create a more inclusive deployment workflow

Releases should not feel like high-risk operations handled by a few individuals. They should be predictable, collaborative, and well-governed processes that empower the entire team.

That’s the experience we’re working toward.
